Welcome to Principles of One Health

WFA 4863/6863 · Fall 2026 · Lectures Mon & Wed 12:00–12:50 · Lab Wed 2:00–3:50

One planet, one system, one health. Everything in health is connected — forests to fevers, markets to microbes, policy to pathogens. This course teaches you to see the connections and trace the mechanisms: how human, animal, and environmental health shape each other, and where to intervene. You won't memorize lists; you'll learn to think in systems.

Your instructor

Dr. Manuel Ruiz Aravena is a disease ecologist whose research focuses on how pathogens circulate in wildlife and cross into domestic animals and people — the same questions this course will teach you to ask.

Questions? Email mir41@msstate.edu (replies within 24 hours on business days) or book office hours through the link in Announcements on Canvas.

The course in three arcs

  • 1 · Foundations — Weeks 1–5. Systems thinking, ecosystem services, social determinants, climate, food & water. Exam 1 in Week 5.
  • 2 · Zoonotic Interfaces — Weeks 6–11. Spillover, wildlife reservoirs, outbreaks, surveillance, risk, and AMR. Exam 2 in Week 11.
  • 3 · Making It Real — Weeks 12–15. Governance, collaboration, risk communication, policy — ending with your presentations.

The rhythm of every week

  • Monday, 12:00–12:50 — first concept of the week.
  • Wednesday, 12:00–12:50 — second concept, and how the two connect.
  • Wednesday lab, 2:00–3:50 — hands-on practice making systems visible: mapping, searching, synthesizing, communicating.
  • Any time before Friday — post your Systems Check: one traced pathway, one diagram, one written description.
