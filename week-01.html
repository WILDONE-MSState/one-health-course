Welcome to One Health — Orientation & Toolkit

Welcome. Everything in health is connected, and this short first week is about getting you equipped to study those connections. One lecture to frame what One Health is and how this course will work, and one lab to set up the four tools that become your workbench for the rest of the semester.

By Friday you can…

  1. Name the pillars of One Health — human, domestic-animal & wildlife, and environmental health — and explain why they cannot be separated
  2. Describe how this course runs — readings before class, discussion in class, study guides delivered after
  3. Have all four toolkit apps installed, configured, and connected to your team workspace
  4. Create your first linked note in your Obsidian vault

Introductions: your corner of One Health

Tell us who you are, where you come from academically, and which corner of One Health pulls you in — wildlife, livestock, people, ecosystems, policy? In two or three sentences, describe one health problem you personally find fascinating. Then read your classmates' posts: the person whose corner is farthest from yours is probably your most valuable teammate this semester.

Laboratory · Wed 2:00–3:50

Toolkit Setup — your One Health workbench

In your teams, install and configure the four tools we use all semester: Obsidian (your course notebook), Paperpile (readings and citations), Excalidraw (systems diagrams), and Google Drive (team workspace). Deliverable by end of session: your team Drive folder created and shared with the teaching team, and your first Obsidian note posted.

How to use the interactive explainer

The interactive explainer linked above is a curated baseline — a starting map of One Health assembled for this course. Read through it before Wednesday, but don't stop at reading: the page is interactive, so ask it the questions that occur to you as you go. Where does an answer feel incomplete? What would you ask next? Bring one question it couldn't fully answer to Wednesday's discussion — that's exactly the kind of curiosity this course runs on.

Wednesday's lab

We'll work through the Lab 1 guide — Installing Your Toolkit together, in teams. Skim it beforehand if you want a head start; everything gets installed and tested during the session, ending with your first annotated paper in Paperpile.
